A card's body is a rich-text document with its own history. Press it to edit; the formatting menu
appears over the selection, and the browser's usual shortcuts work.

Typing `/` at the start of a line opens the insert menu. It lists the same formatting commands the
toolbar does — bold, italic, headings, lists, quotes, code, a divider — plus the things that only
insert: media, an embed of another card, a mention, a property block. One table feeds all three
surfaces, so a command cannot exist in the menu and be missing from the toolbar.

Headings are one row in that menu and one button in the toolbar, each opening a list: six levels and
a paragraph to return to. Typing `/h3` and pressing Enter still applies H3 directly, because a
reader who knows what they want should not have to open a menu to say it.

Text edits are collaborative in the same way structure is: two people can type in one card, and the
result is a merge rather than a last-write-wins surprise.

## Adding media

`/media` in a body and **New media…** on the canvas open the same picker, and the picker is the only place a kind is
chosen: an image, a video, a book, a YouTube embed, or a picture by link. What differs is where the answer lands. At a
caret it is inserted where you are writing; from the canvas it becomes a card of the media type at the point you
pressed, with the block inside it.

Both paths ask for the file first and make the card after, so cancelling the dialog leaves nothing on the board.
