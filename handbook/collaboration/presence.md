title: People and presence
description: "Cursors, names, avatars, and what is shared about where you are."
order: 10
---

Other people are drawn above the board and below the chrome. A cursor is a pointer with a name, in
flow coordinates, so it sits on the right card at every zoom. A name tag labels the thing someone is
manipulating, and avatars on a card collapse to a count when a crowd gathers.

Presence includes what a person is doing, not only where their pointer is: which card they are
focused on, what they have selected, and — if they are presenting — what they are pointing at. That
is what makes following someone possible and what a tour's spotlight is built on.

A hover is not presence. Reading an article, resting on a mention, or opening a peek publishes
nothing, so one person's pointer crossing another's work cannot move anything on their screen.

Presence is for a shared workspace only. Two windows looking at the same local file have nothing to
say to each other, and a cursor nobody receives is not worth sending.

The people here are drawn beside the sync pill: a dot per person, with their name on hover. Somebody
reading a different board is still *here* — the cluster says "elsewhere in this workspace" rather than
hiding them — because being in a workspace with another person and not knowing it is worse than any
clutter. Cursors themselves only appear on the board they belong to.

A person who closes their laptop leaves no goodbye behind. Their dot fades after about fifteen
seconds of silence, which is also how a crash looks. That is deliberate: a presence signal that
needed an announcement would be a presence signal that lies when the announcement never comes.
