# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to make the same thing better. Argue
about the work, not the person.

## Expected behaviour

- Critique the change, the design or the evidence, not the contributor.
- Ask before assuming. "What did you mean by this?" costs one message.
- Accept that a maintainer's decision on scope is a decision, and that "not in
  this beta" is a legitimate answer.
- Give credit where it is due, and say when you were wrong.
- Keep review comments about what you want to see change; a review is not a
  place to keep score.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained disruption of a discussion.
- Insults, personal attacks, or comments about someone's identity, background
  or experience.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information without their consent.
- Dismissing a report of the above, or retaliating against the person who made
  it.

## Scope

This covers the repository: issues, pull requests, review comments, commit
messages and any project space used for the project. It applies to maintainers
and contributors equally.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report a problem to the maintainer listed on the repository profile, privately.
Reports are read by the maintainer alone. A first response is acknowledgement
that the report arrived; what follows depends on the report:

- A private word with the person, when the behaviour is a one-off.
- A request to stop and a cooling-off period, when it is not.
- Removal from the project (review rights, issue access, or all participation) when it
  continues, or when the behaviour is serious on its own.

Exactly what happened, and what the reporter wants, decides which of those
applies. A maintainer involved in the report steps out of the decision.

There is no escalation ladder beyond the maintainer while the project is
single-maintainer; that is a limitation of the beta, not a policy.
